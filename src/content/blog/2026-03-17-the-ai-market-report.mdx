someone ran 4 lead magnets across x and linkedin over the past few months. 242 business owners filled out detailed forms about their ai challenges, goals, and budgets. the data would cost $10,000+ from a research firm. here's what it says — and more importantly, what it means if you're a founder trying to figure out your next move.

i'm going to give you the numbers and then tell you what they actually mean for your business.

---

## who filled out the forms

before you dismiss this as a sample of small bloggers: 37% of respondents do $500K+ in annual revenue. 21% do $5M+. 62% have 2-10 employees. 24% have 11-50.

these are real businesses with real budgets. not hobbyists.

the industry breakdown matters:
- **31% professional services** — legal, finance, consulting
- **18% saas and tech**
- **14% real estate**
- **11% e-commerce**
- the rest split across hospitality, manufacturing, healthcare

professional services dominated. we'll come back to why that matters.

---

## the real problem isn't what you think

here's the headline stat: **52% said their #1 challenge is "i don't know where to start or what to prioritize."**

not:
- "i can't afford it" (4% said budget was a blocker)
- "ai doesn't work" (61% have already tried it)
- "my team won't adopt it"

just: *where do i start?*

that's the market. half of all business owners with real budgets and real intent are stuck not because they're skeptical — but because they're overwhelmed.

**only 4% said budget was a blocker.** read that again. these companies have money. what they don't have is clarity and bandwidth.

22% said time was their blocker. 13% said literally nothing is holding them back — they just need someone to execute.

the opportunity isn't in convincing people ai works. the education phase is over. the execution gap is where the entire opportunity lives.

---

## small companies and big companies need completely different things

this is where the data gets nuanced.

**$1K-$5K budget companies:** 57% don't know where to start. they're overwhelmed. they need a guide, a roadmap, a starter system. they're buying clarity.

**$15K+ budget companies:** only 22% don't know where to start. their real problems are team buy-in (22%), justifying ROI to stakeholders (16%), and internal alignment. they've already decided ai is the direction. they need help selling it internally and executing it properly.

these are completely different sales conversations. if you're pitching the same way to both, you're losing one of them — probably the bigger one.

the $15K+ buyer isn't asking "does this work?" they're asking "can you prove it, integrate it, and help me get my team on board?" that's a trust sale, not an education sale.

---

## what they actually want from ai

**44% want to automate internal processes, save time, and cut costs.** only 18% specifically mentioned revenue growth.

the primary desire isn't "make me more money." it's "stop wasting my time."

this has real implications for how you position ai services:

- "we'll save you 10 hours a week" lands harder than "we'll grow your revenue"
- "automate your intake process" lands harder than "scale your business"
- efficiency is the lead, growth is the outcome

when asked what success looks like in 6 months, 31% described automation and efficiency. 13% named specific processes — crm automation, lead gen, reporting, customer service. they're not dreaming about agi. they want practical, measurable workflow improvements they can point to.

---

## the 25% that nobody is talking about

61% have already tried ai. 25% tried it and got limited results.

**that 25% is the opportunity.**

they already believe. they already invested time and probably money. it didn't work — not because ai doesn't work, but because implementation was wrong. now they're actively looking for someone who can actually deliver.

they're not skeptics. they're burned believers. and burned believers who find the right partner convert at a much higher rate than cold prospects because they've already done their own education. they just need someone who can execute correctly.

if you're an ai implementation partner, your best leads are the people who tried chatgpt for three weeks, got generic results, and concluded "maybe it's not for us." they're one good demo away from becoming your best clients.

---

## the budget data is more interesting than the headline

72% said $1K-$5K. that's the majority. but look closer:

- **54 companies said $5K+**
- **25 said $15K+**
- **8 said $50K+**

and here's the part that should make you pay attention: **the researcher estimated $1.2M-$2.8M+ in pipeline value from 193 organic leads. zero ad spend. zero cold outreach. just content and lead magnets.**

5 discovery calls were booked directly from the typeform. all 5 at $15K+ budgets. combined pipeline $75K-$250K+. companies ranging from 51 to 200+ employees. revenues from $5M to $100M+.

the lead magnet itself pre-qualified the buyers. by the time they filled out the form, they already trusted the person running it enough to share their budget. that's the content flywheel working correctly.

---

## the vertical breakdown that matters

**professional services (31%):** they sell hours. ai saves hours. the roi math is a single sentence. this is the easiest vertical to sell ai implementation to because you don't have to explain abstract value — you calculate it directly.

**real estate (14%):** 60% don't know where to start. 20% said "all of the above" when asked what they want. this industry is early and overwhelmingly needs guidance. low competition, high intent.

**manufacturing (6% of leads, but):** some of the highest-revenue companies in the entire dataset. $100M+ firms. massively underpenetrated by ai agencies. most consultants avoid manufacturing because it seems complex — which means less competition and bigger contracts for the people willing to go there.

---

## what this actually means if you're a kingdom-minded founder

**1. you don't need to convince anyone ai works.** the education phase is over. 61% have already tried it. your job is to give them a clear path forward and prove you've done this before.

**2. lead with time saved, not revenue generated.** 44% want efficiency. 18% want growth. meet them where they are.

**3. your content is your qualification filter.** 54 companies at $5K+ budgets came from organic content alone. the trust is built before they ever reach out. every article is a lead magnet. every framework you share is a demonstration that you know what you're doing.

**4. the $15K+ buyer needs a different conversation.** they're not asking "does this work?" they're asking "can i trust you to execute this inside my organization?" that's a relationship sale. it requires proof of work, case studies, and someone they can see themselves working with.

**5. the burned believers are your best prospects.** find the people who already tried ai, didn't get results, and concluded "maybe it's not for us." they need a better implementation, not more education.

---

## what to take away

- **52% of business owners just need a roadmap.** they're not price shopping. they're direction shopping.
- **only 4% mentioned budget as a blocker.** money isn't the problem. clarity and trust are.
- **the execution gap is the entire opportunity.** everyone knows ai works. almost nobody knows how to implement it properly.
- **professional services is the easiest entry point.** they sell time. you save time. one-line pitch.
- **content builds the trust before the conversation starts.** 54 companies at $5K+ came from organic content. no ads. no cold calls.

the market isn't confused about whether ai works. they're confused about where to start and who to trust.

if you can give them a clear roadmap and prove you've done this before — the data says they'll pay you.
