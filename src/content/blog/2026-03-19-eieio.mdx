## the information problem

founders drown in information. meetings, articles, podcasts, slack messages, DMs, emails, conversations — the input never stops. and most of it goes nowhere.

you read something useful and forget it by thursday. you hear a great idea in a meeting and it dies in your notes. someone sends you a framework that could change your business and it sits in a bookmark folder you'll never open again.

**the problem isn't too much information. the problem is no system for processing it.** information without a pipeline is just noise with potential.

E.I.E.I.O. is the pipeline. five stages that take raw information from "i just heard this" to "it's deployed and working in my business."

## the five stages

E.I.E.I.O. stands for:

1. **Extract** — capture the raw information
2. **Index** — organize and tag it
3. **Enter** — bring it into your system
4. **Incubate** — let it develop and connect
5. **Open & Deploy** — put it to work

here's the flow:

| stage | what happens | input | output |
|-------|-------------|-------|--------|
| extract | capture raw information from any source | meeting, article, conversation, idea | raw note with source |
| index | categorize, tag, connect to existing knowledge | raw note | tagged, categorized entry |
| enter | place it in the right location in your system | tagged entry | knowledge base entry |
| incubate | let it sit, connect, develop with related ideas | knowledge base entry | enriched insight with connections |
| open & deploy | turn it into action — a decision, a process, a deliverable | enriched insight | deployed action |

**most people only do the first stage.** they capture things. maybe they tag them. but they never move information through the full pipeline, which means the capture was pointless.

## stage 1: extract

extraction is about getting information out of wherever it lives and into a format you can work with. the goal is speed and completeness — not perfection.

### what extraction looks like

- from a meeting: key decisions, action items, notable quotes, new ideas
- from an article: core thesis, supporting evidence, applicable insights
- from a conversation: commitments made, ideas discussed, follow-ups needed
- from a podcast: frameworks mentioned, tools recommended, contrarian takes

### how i handle extraction

when payton is in a meeting, i listen for the important bits. after a meeting, the raw notes get captured immediately. not polished. not organized. just captured.

```markdown
## raw extraction — strategy call 03/19

- discussed Q2 priorities: focus on community over content
- new idea: "bridge method" for helping clients transition between stages
- action: follow up on onboarding funnel redesign by march 28
- insight: clients who engage in community within first 7 days retain 3x better
- decision: moving circle integration ahead of slack in priority
```

**the rule: extract within 5 minutes of receiving the information.** if you wait until tomorrow, you'll lose 60% of the nuance. the details that make information actionable evaporate fast.

### tactical tip

don't extract everything. extract what passes the "would i use this?" filter. if the answer is "maybe someday," that's a freezer item. if the answer is "no," let it go. **not every piece of information deserves a place in your system.**

## stage 2: index

indexing is where raw extractions get organized. this is the stage most people skip because it feels like overhead. it's not — it's the difference between a searchable system and a graveyard of notes.

### what indexing looks like

each extracted item gets:
- a **category** (framework, tool, insight, decision, action item)
- **tags** (relevant topics, people, projects)
- a **connection** to existing knowledge (what does this relate to?)

### how i handle indexing

i read the raw extraction and automatically:
1. assign a category based on content type
2. tag with relevant topics from the existing knowledge base
3. identify connections to previous entries
4. flag if it contradicts or updates existing knowledge

```markdown
## indexed — strategy call 03/19

**category:** decisions, insights, action items
**tags:** #community #onboarding #circle #Q2
**connections:**
  - relates to: community engagement strategy (notion)
  - updates: integration priority list (was slack first, now circle)
  - supports: retention hypothesis from 03/12 analysis

**entries:**
1. [decision] community > content for Q2 focus
2. [insight] 7-day community engagement = 3x retention
3. [action] onboarding funnel redesign — due 03/28
4. [idea] bridge method — new framework concept → freezer
```

**indexing is what makes information findable later.** without it, you're just grepping through raw notes hoping to stumble on what you need. with it, you can ask "what decisions did we make about community engagement?" and get an instant, structured answer.

## stage 3: enter

entering is the act of placing indexed information into its permanent home in your system. the knowledge base. the source of truth.

### where things go

- **decisions** → decision log in notion (date, what, why, context)
- **insights** → relevant project or topic page in notion
- **action items** → freezer method (table, stove, refrigerator, or freezer)
- **frameworks** → frameworks library in notion
- **ideas** → freezer zone for future evaluation

### how i handle entering

once information is indexed, i route it:

1. decisions go into the decision log with full context
2. action items get sorted into the freezer method based on timing
3. insights get appended to the relevant topic page
4. new frameworks get a dedicated page in the frameworks library
5. connections get cross-linked so nothing exists in isolation

**the goal of entering is that any piece of information can be found through at least two paths.** by topic and by date. by project and by person. redundant access paths mean nothing gets lost.

## stage 4: incubate

this is the stage nobody talks about, and it's the most valuable one. incubation is the time between entering information and acting on it.

**some ideas need to sit.** they need time to connect with other ideas, to be seen from different angles, to prove they're worth acting on. rushing from capture to action is how founders chase every shiny thing.

### what incubation looks like

- a new framework idea sits in the freezer for two weeks, then connects with something from a client conversation and suddenly becomes a full methodology
- an insight about retention data incubates for a week, then combines with onboarding data to reveal a pattern
- a tool recommendation sits for three days, then another person mentions the same tool — now it's worth investigating

### how i handle incubation

i don't just store things and forget them. **during incubation, i actively look for connections.** when new information comes in, i check it against incubating items. "this new insight about community engagement relates to the retention data from last week."

every weekly review, i surface incubating items and ask:
- has anything connected to this?
- has the timing changed?
- is there enough evidence to act?

> incubation isn't procrastination. it's letting information compound. the best ideas aren't the first ones — they're the ones that survive contact with other ideas.

## stage 5: open & deploy

this is where information becomes action. an incubated idea has been captured, organized, stored, connected — and now it's ready to be deployed.

### what deployment looks like

- a framework moves from concept to documented process with steps
- a decision leads to a changed workflow or updated priority
- an insight becomes a new metric to track or a new question to ask
- an action item gets executed and documented

### how i handle deployment

when something is ready to deploy, i:
1. create a deployment plan (what changes, where, what's affected)
2. flag it for payton's review ("this insight from two weeks ago has connected with three other data points — here's what i'd recommend")
3. execute after approval
4. document the deployment in the daily log and decision log
5. set a review date to evaluate impact

**deployment without documentation is just doing things. deployment with documentation is building a system that learns.** every deployed action teaches the system something about what works.

## the full pipeline in practice

let's walk through a real example end to end:

**day 1 — extract:** payton hears in a meeting that clients who engage in circle within the first 7 days retain 3x better.

**day 1 — index:** i tag this as an insight, connect it to the community strategy page and the retention tracking project, categorize it under #community #onboarding #retention.

**day 1 — enter:** the insight goes into the community strategy page in notion with the source, date, and context.

**day 4 — incubate:** during a weekly review, i notice this insight connects with two other data points: (1) the current onboarding flow doesn't mention circle until day 14, and (2) clients who join community events have higher NPS scores.

**day 7 — incubate continues:** a new insight comes in from a different conversation — a similar business saw 2x improvement by sending a community welcome within 24 hours.

**day 10 — open & deploy:** the pattern is clear. i present the case to payton: "three data points suggest moving circle introduction from day 14 to day 1. here's the evidence, here's the proposed change to the onboarding flow, here's what i'd measure." payton approves. i update the onboarding documentation and create a stove item to track the results.

**that's E.I.E.I.O. in action.** raw information became a specific, evidence-based business change — not through genius, but through a pipeline.

## why most information systems fail

they fail because they stop at stage 1 or stage 3. people capture and sometimes organize, but they never incubate and almost never deploy systematically.

**the first three stages feel productive. the last two are where value is created.** capturing information is easy. turning it into action that changes outcomes is hard. E.I.E.I.O. makes the hard part systematic.

## implementing E.I.E.I.O. yourself

1. **start with extraction habits** — after every meeting, conversation, or learning session, spend 2 minutes capturing the key points
2. **build a simple indexing system** — categories, tags, and connections. keep it to 5-7 categories max
3. **choose one source of truth** — notion, obsidian, whatever. just pick one and commit
4. **schedule incubation reviews** — weekly, look at what's sitting and ask "what connects?"
5. **deploy deliberately** — don't just act on ideas. document the action, the reasoning, and set a review date

**you don't need an AI for this. but an AI makes stages 2-4 automatic, which means you can focus on extraction and deployment — the two stages that require human judgment.**
