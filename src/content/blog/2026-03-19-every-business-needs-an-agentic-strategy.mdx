*from the desk of mr. wallace · march 19, 2026 · mr/strategy · 5 min read*

## the hook

if you don't have an agentic ai strategy in 2026, you're making the same mistake as the service business that just built their first website last year.

this week at [NVIDIA](https://nvidia.com)'s GTC 2026 conference, [Jensen Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen_Huang) said it plainly: "every company in the world today needs to have an OpenClaw strategy, an agentic system strategy." that's not a prediction. that's a line in the sand.

## the story

picture a founder — seven-figure business, solid team, sharp operator. she's using [ChatGPT](https://openai.com) to draft emails and summarize meeting notes. she thinks she's using AI.

she's not. she's using a very expensive autocomplete tool.

meanwhile, her competitor just deployed an agent that monitors their CRM, flags stale deals, drafts follow-up sequences, and pings the sales rep — all without a human touching it. **that's not the future. that's this quarter.**

## the challenge

most founders are stuck in chatbot mode. they ask, it answers. they copy, they paste. they move on. that's not a strategy — that's a productivity hack dressed up as transformation.

**the "i'll deal with it later" crowd is going to get lapped.** [McKinsey](https://mckinsey.com) has been saying for two years that AI adoption gaps compound fast — early movers don't just get ahead, they restructure the cost of doing business in ways late movers can't catch up to without pain.

a chatbot waits for your question. an agent works while you sleep. the difference isn't incremental — it's structural.

## how to implement

start with one workflow. not ten. one. here's how:

1. **pick one repetitive, decision-light process** — lead follow-up, invoice reminders, meeting prep summaries, intake forms. something your team does every week that looks the same every time.
2. **map the steps on paper** — what triggers it? what data does it need? what's the output? who needs to be notified? write it out like you're training a new hire.
3. **choose a platform** — [Make](https://make.com), [Zapier](https://zapier.com), or [n8n](https://n8n.io) for workflow automation. for more intelligent agents that reason and act, look at [OpenAI](https://openai.com)'s Assistants API or agent frameworks built on top of it.
4. **connect your tools** — CRM, inbox, calendar, Slack. agents are only as useful as the systems they can touch. start with two integrations, not twenty.
5. **run it in parallel first** — let the agent do the work while a human watches. compare outputs for two weeks. when the error rate is acceptable, cut the human out of the loop.
6. **measure it** — time saved, leads touched, follow-ups sent. if you can't measure it, you can't scale it.

that's one workflow. one agent. then you do it again.

## the principle

> **key principle:** a chatbot answers questions. an agent executes tasks, makes decisions within a defined scope, and reports back. one is a tool. the other is a teammate.

| | chatbot | agent |
|---|---|---|
| **trigger** | you ask it | event-driven (new lead, missed payment, calendar change) |
| **output** | text response | action taken in your systems |
| **memory** | none by default | context-aware across sessions |
| **human required** | yes, every time | only for exceptions |
| **business value** | saves minutes | saves hours, closes gaps |

## what this means for you

agents don't fix broken operations. **a bad process automated at scale is just a faster way to make the same mistake.** if your follow-up sequence is weak, an agent running it will send weak follow-ups to more people, faster.

get your fundamentals right first. clear process, clean data, defined ownership. then deploy an agent into that structure and it multiplies what's already working.

good leaders get leverage. bad leaders get exposure. agents don't change that dynamic — they amplify it.

## what to automate vs. what to protect

> **framework — automate the repeatable, protect the relational:**
>
> **automate:** lead nurture sequences · invoice follow-ups · meeting prep briefs · data entry · reporting · intake routing · appointment reminders
>
> **protect:** sales conversations that close deals · client relationships that drive retention · hiring decisions · strategic pivots · anything that requires judgment built on trust

the rule is simple: if the value comes from the human touch, keep the human. if the value comes from consistency and speed, automate it.

## the close

Jensen Huang didn't say some companies need an agentic strategy. he said every company. **the window to build this before your competitors do is open right now — and it won't stay open.**

pick one workflow this week. build one agent. see what it does to your capacity.
